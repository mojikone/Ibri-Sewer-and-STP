## 1. THE HEADLINE

The built network is **one system with one outfall**, not three — three gravity trees stitched together by three pressure mains, closed at the STP at `5A-1-FL-STP`, with exactly one pumping station in 95.45 km. What it does well is **geometry**: it sets chambers out at 30 m and 35 m on a drawing (140 reaches are exactly 30.00 m, 46 exactly 35.00 m), lays pipes as dead-straight two-point lines (98.13 %), brings branches in square (branch-inlet median 90.0°, IQR 88.6–92.5°), never lets a fourth pipe into a chamber (max fan-in 3, on 3,267 chambers), refuses dual carriageways at roughly 160× the rate chance would give (0.082 % of length within 4 m of a `dual=1` centreline against a 13.32 % availability), holds every tier off primary and secondary roads (trunk 100.00 % on StrCls 05, 0.00 % on 01/02, against 9.2 % availability), and follows the wadi valley while staying out of its channel (trunk 69.5 % on flood class ≥1 but only 1.15 % on class 5–6, against 6.08 % in the roads of its own valley — a 5.3× avoidance on severe hazard). What it does badly is **everything downstream of the plan**: two diameters over 95 km with tier explaining 100 % of diameter variance (ANOVA R² = 1.000000), sub-mains laid **flatter** than the laterals feeding them (4.04 vs 6.03 mm/m median in the same OD160 bore), 43.0 % of levelled length below the 1.30 m minimum cover measured at the shallow end, and 238 reaches / 7.54 km that cannot pass the saturated peak — 63.5 % of the trunk and 45.3 % of the sub-mains. And a third of it is invisible: package **5A-1 (1,123 pipes, 32.245 km, 33.78 %)** carries no diameters, no inverts, no material and no TM/SM tokens, so it cannot be measured at all — and it is the pumped third. **Copy the plan geometry, copy the corridor discipline, copy nothing about size or level, and never pool 5A-1 into any statistic.**

**Conflicts between dimensions — resolve before adopting either rule.**

1. **Road class field.** One dimension says `StrCls` "carries no information" (99.7 % of everything in class 05); another builds its entire headline on it (trunk 0.00 % on class 01/02, a 34× avoidance). **The second is right.** 99.7 % is the share of the **pipe** in class 05, not of the classification — by length **9.2 % of road in the network's own neighbourhood is class 01/02**. Keep `StrCls`.
2. **Where a diameter may change.** One dimension proposes "every size change must sit at a junction" as an export gate. Tested: computing the required diameter at each reach's own laid gradient, **the built network's own geometry would demand 87 size changes, 15 of them mid-run at a plain pass-through chamber**. A design that sizes honestly to accumulated flow *will* cross a capacity band between junctions on a long run. **Monotonicity is the hard gate; junction placement is a reporting check with a stated allowance.**
3. **Flow-versus-diameter ordering as an export gate.** Capacity goes as D^2.67·S^0.5, so a steeper small pipe legitimately carries more than a flatter large one. Tested on the built network: in package 5A-3 the rule flags **16 OD160 reaches that pass their own Colebrook check — 16 of the 38 it flags network-wide, 42 % false positives.** Keep `DIA(reach) ≤ DIA(successor)` as the gate; make the load half a **utilisation audit (q/Qcap ≤ 1)**.
4. **Steep ground.** "Raise the gradient to 9–11 mm/m" is measured and safe. "Accept that cover trends to the minimum by the bottom of the slope" is the **mechanism that put 44.6 % of steep-down length (50.3 % on DEM-steep ground) below 1.30 m** — the same breach flagged DO-NOT-COPY. **Rewrite as: raise the gradient AND trigger a drop structure before cover reaches 1.30 m.** That is a departure from the as-built, not a copy of it. And "take the surplus fall at a chamber" is not what they did — on *absolute* ground gradient drops sit at 6.40 mm/m against 7.52 at flush chambers, a **15 %** difference, not the 2.35× the signed figures (1.62 vs 3.81) imply; the signed comparison measures direction mix, not steepness.
5. **The rising-main summit.** One dimension uses it to argue the static lift is negative (friction duty only); another uses it to argue a larger main would siphon and delete the station. **Both are wrong for the same reason**: the summit at ch 381 sits ~7 m above a 3–7 m wet-well LWL, so **nothing gravitates over it at any diameter**, and above the siphon threshold the main simply runs part-full downstream of the summit and needs **air valves at every high point, washouts at every low point** — the station is not deletable. Measured properly the static lift is **+5.2 to +9.2 m**, not negative, and the route rises above station ground on **two** stretches totalling **834 m (8.3 %)**, not one. Meanwhile at DN300 the friction gradient (5.1 mm/m) exceeds the ground fall downstream of the summit (2.448 mm/m), so the HGL does clear it and the endpoint difference **does** control — the blanket "not endpoint difference" is wrong in exactly the regime it claims applies.

## 6. WHAT COULD NOT BE MEASURED

Named gaps. Nobody should assume these were covered.

1. **Package 5A-1, entirely — 1,123 pipes, 32.245 km, 33.78 % of built length.** `DIA_OUT` null on all 1,123; `HAS_LVL = 0` on all 1,123; `MATERIAL` null on all 1,123; `US_INV` zero on all 1,123; **zero TM and zero SM tokens across 312 zones**. Nothing about its hierarchy, diameter, gradient, cover, drops, descent split or capacity exists. It is also **the catchment that drains to the pumping station**, so the depth-versus-pumping question is unanswerable on exactly the ground where it matters. It corrupts the hierarchy ratio, chain depth, zone density, tier shares, uphill share and wadi exposure **simultaneously** — it holds **89.6 % of the network's class 4–6 hazard length** and moves the network-wide wadi figure by a **factor of seventeen**. **Data request to NWS: 5A-1's as-built pipe schedule AND its tier labels** — without the labels it will keep corrupting every pooled hierarchy statistic, not just the sizing ones.
2. **The rising main's diameter, material and pipe class.** L021671, L012750 and L012751 all carry zero diameter fields and null MATERIAL. The bracket from friction and the 2.5 m/s cap alone is **ID ~205–375 mm (DN250–DN400)** — a full standard size wide once roughness is allowed to be that of a 2006 main. **Highest-value single data request in the set.**
3. **The trunk's internal bore.** `IN_DIAMETE = 0` on all 145 OD200 rows. The saturated failure count is knife-edge on it: **62–67 % across ID 176–192 mm, with 28 of 145 reaches sitting between 0.9 and 1.1 utilisation** at both ID 188 and 192. The defensible statement is "most of the trunk is at or past capacity at saturation on any plausible bore" — **not a count**.
4. **Whether the 90° branch inlet is a drafting rule or the street grid.** Untestable: **32.0 % of built chambers are more than 25 m from any centreline** in `Road_Centercline.shp` (median 8.45 m, p90 74.6 m, p99 255 m). A perpendicular side street gives 90° whether or not anyone decided it.
5. **Whether the built network runs in a road reserve.** Same reason, same layer. Either `Road_Centercline.shp` omits internal residential streets or the pipe genuinely runs off-street — the data cannot separate the two. In 5A-5 the evidence points at the layer: **14.011 km sits more than 25 m from any mapped road, and 80.8 % of it is within 8 m of a plot boundary at a median 2.01 m, against a 57.7 % / 6.48 m chance floor**. Report "3 m clear of the plot boundary", never "in the road reserve".
6. **Whether pipe-inside-plot is a 2006 decision or a cadastre change.** 2006 pipe against a current cadastre. It cuts both ways — plots resurveyed around a wayleave would **remove** pipe from polygons, not add it. Unresolvable from this data; the caveat stays attached to the 7.52 %.
7. **Ground truth for gradient.** Neither surface qualifies. **var(surveyed) = 406.2 vs var(DEM) = 242.8** (sd 20.15 vs 15.58) — the 2006 survey has *more* gradient variance than the raster, so a substantial part of the 16.9 mm/m disagreement is survey error or twenty years of ground change, not DEM noise. The operational conclusion (two-point sampling is unusable) holds either way.
8. **Whether depth triggered the pumping decision.** A shallow maximum (8.69 m) in a network that pumps is **equally consistent with depth being the trigger and with the designer pumping rather than digging**. And PAM-GUD-203 post-dates the 2006 design, so its tables cannot have been the designer's reasoning in the first place.
9. **Connection counts and any commissioning record.** No connection dataset exists. "5,963 properties" does not reproduce; "60.8 km commissioned" has no external source. Measured proxies only: **4,876 saturated properties / 3,645 live ELE accounts** at 60 m.
10. **Load allocation generally — this touches every property, capacity and utilisation number in the study.** 4,175 of 56,414 loaded plots matched within 60 m = **7.40 % of the plot layer**. The worst OD160 carries **1,209 / 1,399 / 1,570 / 1,704** properties at 30 / 60 / 100 / 150 m; zone `5A-1|A` carries **2,621 / 3,028 / 3,337 / 3,625**. **Quote them as "over a thousand saturated properties on an OD160" and "over 2,600 properties through a 468 m zone labelled as an ordinary lateral"** — true at every radius. Publish the radius with the number, always.
11. **Every profile along a schematic line is a chord fiction.** L021671: 41 vertices over 10 km (249.8 m/vertex). L012750: 317 m/vertex. L012751: 565 m/vertex. Cover, hazard and slope sampled along them describe a straight line between vertices, not a trench.
12. **The loop's cause.** One surplus edge in 3,265 (edges − nodes + components = 1, robust to the filter). `5A-2-30-MH235` splits into two branches that rejoin at `5A-2-SM.1-MH226` after **8 pipes / 214.6 m** and **7 pipes / 232.1 m** — **446.7 m of pipe and 13 intermediate chambers**, not the 64 m of the two pipes leaving the split. Whether it is a designed dual-feed or a digitising artefact is unknown; it also carries one of the six negative invert steps. **A 447 m dual-feed is a real thing to resolve on site; a 64 m one is a curiosity — report the loop length, not the split pipes.**
13. **Whether 5A-3's 40 m spacing habit is a rule.** 126 pipes / 3.45 km / **3.6 % of the network**, and the only package that holds it (max 39.90 m). The other four have 49, 48, 35 and 9 reaches over 40 m with maxima of 53.80, 71.38, 65.02 and 51.19 m. **One small package is not a design standard.**
14. **Roundabout behaviour.** 3 islands identified by polygonising three road layers; 1 reached; **2.4 m of pipe inside (0.0025 %)**. Sample far too small to state a rule.
15. **The general methodological gap the whole study exposes.** Every avoidance statistic must be reported **against the road-corridor choice set, never against all ground**. The one dimension that ran that control killed its own hypothesis with it — the built pipe sits on **steeper** ground than its corridor (41.31 % vs 36.06 % above 2 % slope, a 0.87× "avoidance"), and the apparent 9× steep-ground avoidance was entirely an artefact of comparing against all ground. **Put no steep-ground penalty in the W12 routing objective, and cite no as-built authority for one.**
