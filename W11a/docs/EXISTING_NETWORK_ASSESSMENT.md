# The existing network, assessed — the brownfield half of W11a

W11a, 2 September 2026. The user's instruction was to design **ignoring** the built network
first, then assess the built network and model both. This is that assessment.

Everything here is regenerated by `W11a/report/existing_network.py` — run it and every table
below reprints. Nothing in this document is quoted from another document without being
re-measured from the source artefact.

**Sources.** `Data/Received/09-RECEIVED/NAMA/IBRI/WW/SHIP/*.shp` (the ESRI delivery, not the
KMZ, written 2026-08-16 18:20) · `W10/shp/W10_plot_loads.gpkg`, 64,071 plots, written
2026-09-01 10:24 · `W11a/run/s5_reach_skeleton.gpkg`, `W11a/shp/W11a_trunk.gpkg` and
`W11a/shp/W11a_reaches.shp` — the design as it stood on 2026-09-02, afternoon.
**The design side is a moving target.** Other stages were writing those files while this ran:
stages 6 to 9 completed mid-way through, the design length moved 5 km between two runs an
hour apart, and stage 5c's largest accumulated flow moved from 5,538 m³/d to 56,616 m³/d in
forty minutes. **The built-network numbers do not move.** The comparison numbers carry the
timestamps above and should be re-run before they are quoted to a client.

## 0. The four things to read first

**1 — There is almost nothing to reuse, and three quarters of it is lateral.** The design
re-lays **45.0 km of the 95.4 km** that is built. Tested two independent ways — against the
plots that front it (§7) and against what the finished design routes down that street (§8) —
the reusable length is **29 to 37 km**, which is **1.7 to 2.2 % of the design's own
1,732 km**. And the split by what the design wants is absolute: **where the design wants a
lateral, not one built pipe is short; where it wants a trunk main, not one is adequate**, the
median being short by a factor of 47. If NWS also requires reused pipe to be *compliant*, 92.8
% of the built network is OD160 — below the OD200 minimum for a lateral or a main, G203-p22
Tab 6 — and the envelope collapses further. **A brownfield credit will not change this
scheme's cost materially and should not be carried in the appraisal as if it might.**

**2 — Not one built lateral fails, and more than half the built spine does.** Every
capacity failure in the built network sits in the 14.45 km of sub main and trunk main. The
median built **lateral** uses **5 %** of its own capacity at saturation; the median built
**trunk main** pipe uses **101 %**. That is exactly what a network sized for an
1,800 m³/d works looks like when you ask it to carry a town. The collection is generous; the
spine is spent.

**3 — A binding rule in `08_DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY.md` rests on a number that does not
reconcile.** §4 says *"Target tier shares near the as-built: lateral 66 %, sub main 18 %,
trunk 5 %"* and *"Expect roughly 270 km of sub main on this network. A design producing 20 km
is wrong on sight."* The 18.4 % traces to `W8/docs/LEARNING_FROM_ASBUILT.md`, whose own table
gives the sub mains as **~4.0 km of 78.6 km** — which is 5.1 %, not 18.4 %. Both other rows
in that table divide correctly by 78.6 km; the sub-main row does not. Re-measured: the
sub-main share is **6.6 %** inside W8's test boundary and **10.40 %** over the whole built
network. The tripwire should be restated on 10.4 %, which puts the expectation near **180 km**
rather than 270 km. §2 below has the arithmetic.

**4 — The TOR requires this assessment and forbids relying on it.** Scope p4 item 5: *"The
Consultant shall conduct Hydraulic assessment for proposed and Existing system and to prepare
the required upgradation, Rehabilitation and Integrations for entire area."* Scope p7 item
14: *"…information folder … is only for information. The Consultant shall not establish his
design on such information. The Consultant is responsible to collect all required data … and
validate physically."* NAMA's own `REMARKS` field on every built record says the same:
*"Data is not reliable and must be used only for reference purpose."* **Everything below is a
survey specification, not a conclusion.**

## 2. The shape of the built network, and the philosophy correction

Tier comes off the third token of NAMA's own manhole IDs (`5A-2-TM-MH185` trunk,
`5A-2-SM.2-MH391` sub main, `5A-1-A49-MH3` lateral zone A49). Every built ID is exactly four
tokens, so the parse never guesses.

| Tier | Pipes | km | Share | Median run | Diameter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| lateral | 2,798 | 81.00 | **84.86 %** | 29.47 m | OD160 × 1,675 (1,123 unrecorded) |
| sub main | 322 | 9.92 | **10.40 %** | 30.02 m | OD160 × 322 |
| trunk main | 145 | 4.53 | **4.75 %** | 30.00 m | OD200 × 145 |

**The whole built network is OD160 except a 4.53 km trunk at OD200.** G203-p22 Table 6 reads,
verbatim: *"Rider Sewer, Property Connection Sewer* — OD 160 mm (minimal)"; *"Lateral Sewer,
Maximum Length 45 m* — OD 200 mm (minimal)"; *"Main sewer* — OD 200 mm (minimal) to 300 mm".
So **1,997 pipes / 58.67 km — 92.8 % of the dimensioned built network — are laid at the size
the guideline reserves for a rider or a property connection.** The bore is not assumed: the
project's own `internal_diameter()` returns 150.6 mm for OD160 and NAMA's `IN_DIAMETE` field
says 150.

*One caveat on that reading.* The shapefile metadata (`SEWERLINE_IBRI.shp.xml`) carries no
field definition for `OUT_DIAMET`, only edit history, so "OD in millimetres" is **inferred**
— from the field's own name beside `IN_DIAMETE`, from OD160 pairing with ID150 on all 1,997
records (a 5 mm wall on a 160 mm pipe, which is the uPVC SDR34/SN8 wall), and from G203-p22
Table 6 expressing its own minima in OD. The inference is strong but it is an inference, and
a survey settles it. The OD200 pipes carry no `IN_DIAMETE`; their bore is taken as 188.2 mm
from the same SDR34 relation.

**The philosophy's tier target, checked.** W8's test boundary
(`Hydraulic/SHP/temp/Netwrok desing test boudary.shp`, 5.51 km²) reproduces W8's lengths
exactly — trunk 4.017 km, sub main 3.972 km, lateral 52.316 km, 60.31 km in total. W8
published those as 5.1 % / 18.4 % / 66.5 %. 4.02 ÷ 78.6 = 5.1 ✓ and 52.32 ÷ 78.6 = 66.6 ✓,
but 4.0 ÷ 78.6 = **5.1, not 18.4**, and the three published shares sum to 90.0 %. There is no
14.5 km of sub main anywhere in the data.

| Sub-main share | Value |
|---|---:|
| `08_DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY.md` §4 target, from W8 | 18 % → *"roughly 270 km"* |
| measured, W8 test boundary | **6.6 %** |
| measured, whole built network | **10.40 %** |
| W11a design at 2026-09-02 14:40, 1,731.7 km | 15.41 % (266.8 km) |

**This does not mean the design is wrong.** The two vocabularies differ: NAMA has no "main"
tier, W11a does. Fold the design's `main` into `lateral` and the comparison becomes
79.7 / 15.4 / 4.9 against the built 84.9 / 10.4 / 4.75 — the trunk share matches almost
exactly and the sub-main share is about half again as high, which is defensible for a network
carrying 13× the load. The finding is narrower and sharper than "the design is wrong": **the
270 km expectation and the "20 km is wrong on sight" tripwire come from a number that does not
reconcile with its own table, and should be restated on the measured 10.4 %.**

**It is three systems, not one.** The manhole graph is a clean DAG: 3,267 nodes, 3,265 edges,
no cycles, and exactly **three sinks**. There is no gravity connection between them.

| Outfall node | Package | km upstream | Today | 2055 | Ultimate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `5A-1-FL-SPS` (to the pumping station and the 9.99 km rising main) | 5A-1 | 32.2 | 2,067 | 2,149 | 2,354 m³/d |
| `5A-5-TM-MH1759` (to the works via the 5.6 km schematic line) | 5A-5 | 34.7 | 1,350 | 1,538 | 2,013 m³/d |
| `5A-4-SM-MH917` (to the works via the 10.5 km 8F-1 line) | 5A-4 (+5A-2, 5A-3) | 28.5 | 1,167 | 1,239 | 1,420 m³/d |
| **all three** | | **95.4** | **4,584** | **4,926** | **5,787 m³/d** |

**One topology anomaly:** manhole `5A-2-30-MH235` carries two outgoing pipes whose upstream
inverts differ by 1.98 m. One chamber cannot have two outlets 2 m apart, so one of the two
records is mis-attributed. Flow is routed down the lower outlet here and the node is reported
rather than averaged away.

## 4. The load on it, and the works

Plots are put on the built network by proximity: a plot is served if its boundary is within
**45 m** of a built pipe, which is G203-p22 Table 6's own maximum lateral length used as a
service radius. The load is then routed on NAMA's manhole topology, not on geometry.

| | Plots | Today (CLASS B + U) | Ultimate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| within 25 m | 4,186 | 4,350 | 5,466 m³/d |
| **within 45 m (used)** | **4,458** | **4,584** | **5,787 m³/d** |
| within 60 m | 4,625 | 4,707 | 6,038 m³/d |
| within 100 m | 4,972 | 4,978 | 6,466 m³/d |

The answer is not sensitive to the radius: quadrupling it moves the ultimate load by 12 %.
The built network fronts **7.7 % of the 74,701 m³/d in the boundary**.

**The existing works is rated 1,800 m³/d and 4,584 m³/d of already-developed load fronts the
built network — 2.5× the plant.** Either the plant is heavily overloaded, or a large part of
the 95 km is not connected or not commissioned. Nothing in this dataset can tell you which,
and it is the single most useful thing a client meeting could settle.

**Horizons.** *Today* is measured — the sum of `Q_AVG_M3D` over plots of CLASS B (built) and U
(unparceled buildings). *Ultimate* is measured — all plots. *2055* is a **PROJECT ASSUMPTION**,
not a measurement: the share of today's undeveloped stock developed by 2055 is taken as
(237,885 − 159,162) ÷ (435,945 − 159,162) = **0.284**, from `W10/docs/LOAD_ALLOCATION.md` §6
and §7 (built population today 144,325 + 14,837; cadastral saturation 435,945; NCSI/R0 2055
projection 237,885). **It assumes growth in the built network's catchment tracks the whole
boundary**, which `W3/A2` explicitly contradicts — IBRI is projected to lose about 43,000
people to AT TAYYIB by 2055. In the old town the assumption is probably generous. It is also
the least important number here, because §5 shows the horizon barely moves the answer.

**Peak flow** is Merrimack, `Qpdf = 2.65 Qadf^0.879` in Ml/d, mandatory above 100 properties
and held at the 100-property value below it (G201-p71 §7.4.2) — the same rule
`s5c_flows.py` applies. **Infiltration is 10 % of flow, not 720 L/d/km.** G201-p72 §7.4.3
gives three rates and the pipeline uses only the third: *"For existing networks located within
groundwater table zones or coastal areas, an infiltration rate of up to 40 % of the wastewater
flow should be considered. For existing networks located inland or outside groundwater table
influence, an infiltration rate of 10 % should be applied. For newly designed networks, a
linear infiltration allowance of 720 litres per day per kilometre (L/d/km) of sewer should be
incorporated."* Ibri is inland at ~330 m aOD, so **10 % governs the built network and
720 L/d/km governs the new design.** The choice changes the answer by 10 pipes and 0.34 km
(§5), so it is a correctness point rather than a material one — but the 40 % groundwater case
is not, and nothing in this dataset says where the water table is.

## 5. Capacity, at three horizons

Capacity is the flow at the G203-p27 Table 10 depth-of-flow limit — **d/D ≤ 0.65 for a pipe
diameter up to 350 mm** — computed with the project's own validated hydraulics
(`W8/py/sewnet/hydra.py`, Colebrook-White, kₛ = 1.5 mm per G203-p24 and p28), on the gradient
derived from NAMA's own inverts and the bore behind NAMA's own OD.

| Horizon | Pass | Over d/D | **Surcharged** | Untested | Fail, % of the testable 63.20 km |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| today, plots developed now | 57.34 km | 1.16 km | **4.70 km** | 32.25 km | **9.3 %** |
| 2055 (interpolated) | 56.95 km | 1.41 km | 4.85 km | 32.25 km | 9.9 % |
| ultimate / saturation | 56.09 km | 1.45 km | **5.66 km** | 32.25 km | **11.3 %** |

**Growth is not what breaks it.** 5.86 km of the eventual 7.11 km of failure — **82 %** —
is already there at today's developed load. Where it sits is the finding:

| Tier | km | Testable km | Fails at ultimate | % of testable | Median pipe, share of its own capacity used |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| lateral | 81.00 | 48.75 | **0.00 km** | **0 %** | **0.05** |
| sub main | 9.92 | 9.92 | 4.49 km | 45.2 % | 0.68 |
| trunk main | 4.53 | 4.53 | 2.63 km | **58.0 %** | **1.01** |

**Not one built lateral fails at any horizon, and the median one uses a twentieth of its
capacity.** The whole problem is 14.45 km of spine. The worst pipes are three OD160 sub-main
reaches in 5A-5 carrying 1,092 m³/d (34.4 L/s peak) through a pipe good for 7.1 L/s at the
d/D limit and 9.4 L/s full bore, and a run of OD200 trunk main in 5A-4 at 0.3 % carrying
34.2 L/s against a d/D-limit capacity of 12.6 L/s and a full bore of 16.6 L/s — 2.7x its
design capacity and 2.1x its full bore.

**Sensitivity.**

| Load | Infiltration rule | Pipes over d/D | km |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| half saturation | existing inland, 10 % (used) | 122 | 3.99 |
| half saturation | new network, 720 L/d/km (what s5c applies) | 121 | 3.95 |
| saturation | existing inland, 10 % (used) | 223 | 7.12 |
| saturation | new network, 720 L/d/km | 213 | 6.78 |

**These numbers are a lower bound on the failure, for three reasons.** Flow is routed through
NAMA's own topology, so a pipe does carry what is upstream *of it inside the built network* —
but nothing from beyond it, and any reuse in the new scheme would send more through than
that (§8 shows the extreme case). They assume the 2006 pipe is hydraulically as-new, with no
silt, no root intrusion and no displaced joint, on a dataset that records no condition at
all. And 32.25 km cannot be tested.

Reporting only. A 2006 asset is not condemned by a 2024 guideline. These rows exist because
they say what the survey has to measure and what an adoption case has to argue. Measured on

G203-p33 §4.6.3, verbatim: *"The minimum depth for sewer pipes shall be 1.3 m to the crown of

an OD160 sewer**, because the guideline does not contemplate one as a lateral or main — so the
26.4 % figure is measured against the DN200 row as the nearest applicable value, which is our
choice, not the guideline's.

Median laid gradient on the built network is **6.00 mm/m**. `W7/docs/CALIBRATION_vs_EXISTING.md`
quotes 4.98 mm/m for the as-built. That difference is unexplained and W7's figure could not be
reproduced from any subset tried here; the 6.00 mm/m is what the 2,142 invert-bearing pipes
give.

## 8. The decisive test — can the built pipe carry what the design routes down its street?

Section 5 asks whether a built pipe can carry the plots that front it. This asks whether it
can carry what the **design** sends through it, which is the question a reuse decision
actually turns on.

Stages 6–9 completed while this assessment was being written, so
`W11a/shp/W11a_reaches.shp` now carries a diameter and a peak flow on every reach — 49,377
reaches, 1,731.7 km, DN200–1200, 1,076 L/s at the largest. Each built pipe is matched to the
co-located design reach with the **largest** peak flow inside the 12 m band, because a reuse
decision is governed by the worst thing that street has to carry. Built pipe with a design
reach within 12 m: **2,384 pipes, 69.90 km**.

| What the design wants on that street | Built pipes | Built km | **Adequate** | **Short** | Untestable | Median shortfall | Worst |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| a lateral | 1,446 | 41.52 | **25.15 km** | **0.00 km** | 16.25 km | — | — |
| a main | 561 | 16.77 | 11.59 km | 0.88 km | 4.27 km | 1.09× | 2.52× |
| a sub main | 281 | 8.67 | **0.75 km** | **4.47 km** | 3.45 km | 2.17× | 7.52× |
| a trunk main | 96 | 2.95 | **0.00 km** | **1.28 km** | 1.67 km | **47×** | **63×** |

**The answer is completely determined by what the design asks of the street.** Where the
design wants a lateral, the built pipe is adequate everywhere it can be tested and there is
not one short reach. Where the design wants a trunk main, **nothing** is adequate and the
median pipe is short by a factor of 47.

**Reusable on this test: 1,266 pipes, 37.48 km — 39.3 % of the built network and 2.16 % of
the design.** By tier of the built pipe: lateral 28.36 km (5A-5 13.80, 5A-2 11.05, 5A-4 2.26,
5A-3 1.25), sub main 6.74 km, trunk main 2.38 km.

This is more generous than §7's 29.2 km because it tests the built pipe against what the
design routes past it, not against its own frontage catchment, and the design divides
catchments differently from NAMA's 2006 scheme. **Take 29–37 km as the range, and 0 km of it
where the new trunk runs.**

**Two caveats, both making the shortfall worse than it reads.**

*The design's own accumulation is still short at the works.* `W11a_reaches.shp` peaks at
56,616 m³/d and 1,076 L/s; the stage-3 trunk carries 73,442 m³/d and 1,350 L/s on the same
alignment. So the demands in the table are understated by roughly a quarter at the downstream
end.

*And the two published layers disagree about the same edges.* All 754 trunk edge ids appear
in both `W11a_trunk.gpkg [reaches]` and `W11a_reaches.shp`, and **606 of the 754 carry a
different DN in the two files; 744 of 754 differ on QADF by more than 1 %.** Edge `E0000135`
is DN1700 at 73,442 m³/d in the trunk layer and **DN315 at 813 m³/d** in the reaches layer.
That is not a rounding difference, it is two published answers for one pipe, and it is a
stage-6/stage-3 reconciliation for the pipeline owners — reported here, not fixed.

**The trunk sub-test, kept for the record.** Against the stage-3 trunk (754 reaches, 85.55 km,
DN200–1700, 73,442 m³/d at the works), 68 built pipes / 1.53 km lie within 12 m:

## 12. Confidence

**[Certain]** — the status split, the lengths, the tier shares, the attribute completeness, the
three-system topology, the diameters, the gradients derived from NAMA's inverts, the overlay
and distance measurements against the design as it stood at the timestamps in the header, the
DN and flow disagreement between the two published design layers, and the arithmetic in §11.

**[Likely]** — the capacity verdicts. They use the project's own validated hydraulics on the
guideline's own d/D limit, but they rest on NAMA's inverts, which the dataset's own `REMARKS`
field disclaims, and they assume as-new pipe.

**[Guessing]** — the 2055 column, and any statement about *why* 57 % of package 5A-5 has no
design corridor.

**What would change the answer most:** plant inflow records (decision 1), a condition survey
(gap 1), and the draftsman's roads (gap 6). None of the three is a modelling choice.

**What must be re-run before this is quoted to a client:** everything in §7 and §8 — they
compare against a design that was being rewritten while they were measured, and the two
published design layers do not yet agree with each other.
