# What the sewer hierarchy actually is — the generative rules

W10 deep research, 1 September 2026. Source: NAMA's built 2006 network, 3,266 true-gravity
pipes / 101.10 km, read from `W10/shp/W10_existing_built.shp` with the designer's own
`US_MHID`/`DS_MHID` connectivity.

Everything below is regenerated by three scripts in `W10/py/research/`, and every number
has a CSV in `W10/run/research_hierarchy_*.csv`. Nothing here is asserted from a textbook.

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `research_hierarchy.py` | zone anatomy, chaining, promotion, diameters, gradients, packages, wadis, roads |
| `research_asbuilt_check.py` | runs **our** criteria (`W8/py/sewnet/hydra.py`, `criteria.py`) over the as-built; street test; relative elevation; T50 hazard wadi test |
| `research_rule_test.py` | sweeps and tests the candidate rules, applies them to the one package that has no labels |

---

## 0. The uncomfortable part first

**The doctrine W8 built the design on is measured off the one package that has no
hierarchy in it.**

W8's headline was "91 % of laterals drain into another lateral, crossing a median of 11
before reaching a sub main". That statistic is real, but it is dominated by package
**5A-1**, which carries *no* TM or SM token anywhere in its 1,123 pipes. Every one of
its 312 zones therefore parses as a lateral, so of course they chain — there is nothing
else for them to chain into. 5A-1 is also the package with **0 % invert levels**, against
100 % on every other package. [Certain]

Split the measurement and the picture inverts:

| Chain depth: lateral zones crossed before a sub main or trunk | median | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **5A-2 … 5A-5** (270 zones, the packages that ARE labelled) | **2** | 3 | **5** |
| 5A-1 (312 zones, no labels exist) | 17 | 26 | 31 |
| pooled — the figure W8 used | 5 | 24 | 31 |

`research_hierarchy_chain_depth.csv`

| Where a lateral zone drains | 5A-2 … 5A-5 | 5A-1 | pooled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| into another lateral | **73.0 %** | 99.7 % | 87.3 % |
| into a sub main | **22.2 %** | 0 | 10.3 % |
| into the trunk | **4.8 %** | 0 | 2.2 % |

`research_hierarchy_zone_outflow.csv`

So the real as-built rule is **a lateral chain of about two, never more than five**, not
eleven. [Certain] on the arithmetic; [Likely] that 5A-1 was designed or documented by a
different party, on the joint evidence of the missing tier tokens and the missing levels.

W8's cap-the-joins heuristic reproduced roughly the right *shape* for the wrong reason, and
its own closing note said so. The rules below replace it.

## 1. What makes something a lateral zone

**R1 — A lateral zone is one unbranched run of sewer along one street, from a head chamber
to a single outlet.** [Certain]

This is the strongest single finding in the dataset, and it is topological rather than
statistical.

| | 5A-2 … 5A-5 | all packages |
|---|---:|---:|
| lateral zones that are a **simple unbranched path** (no internal junction, one head) | **99.6 %** | 90.0 % |
| lateral zones with **exactly one outlet** | **99.3 %** | 98.1 % |

`research_hierarchy_zone_topology.csv`. The all-package figure is again dragged down by
5A-1, where a "zone" is sometimes a whole branch system.

The geometry says the same thing — a lateral zone is a **line, not a polygon**:

| Lateral zone geometry | p50 | p95 |
|---|---:|---:|
| convex-hull area | **0.017 ha** | 1.54 ha |
| bearing spread of its pipes | **4.6°** | 43° |
| distinct road centreline features it follows | **2** | 6 |
| sinuosity (laid length ÷ end-to-end) | 1.46 | 4.05 |

52.7 % of lateral zones have a bearing spread under 10°, and 72 % follow two or fewer road
features. `research_hierarchy_zone_street_test.csv`, `..._zone_street_stats.csv`.

**R2 — Size of a lateral zone.** [Certain] Measured on the labelled packages (270 zones):

| | p25 | **p50** | p75 | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| chambers | 2 | **5** | 9 | 17 | 28 |
| length | 49 m | **132 m** | 259 m | 500 m | 916 m |
| properties directly on it | 1 | **4** | 11 | 28 | 97 |
| plots fronting it (60 m) | 3 | **6** | 12 | 29 | 80 |
| chamber spacing within it | 25.4 m | **29.2 m** | 31.7 m | 37.0 m | 40.5 m |

`research_hierarchy_zone_stats.csv`, `..._zone_plots_fronting_stats.csv`.

**A zone is a street, not a block and not a subdivision.** The block reading can be ruled
out directly: MoH_Plots carries no populated `BLOCK_NUMB` at all, so the cadastre cannot
answer it, but a block would show as a compact polygon with a bearing spread near 45° and a
hull of 1–3 ha. The measured hull is 0.017 ha and the bearing spread 4.6°. [Certain]

**R3 — A lateral zone never carries much.** [Certain] In the labelled packages no lateral
pipe anywhere carries more than **136 properties** or **2,467 m** of contributing sewer;
p95 is 53 properties / 1,074 m. `research_hierarchy_chain_ceiling.csv`,
`..._accumulation_by_tier.csv`.

## 2. What makes something a sub main

**R4 — A sub main is a collector route that gathers a cluster of lateral zones and carries
them to the trunk. It is defined by what it delivers, not by what it starts with.**
[Certain]

The negative evidence is decisive: **4 of the 10 sub mains start with zero properties on
them**, and the median load at a sub-main head is 20 properties. There is no head-load
threshold. `research_hierarchy_tier_heads.csv`.

What is consistent is the outlet:

| | min | **p50** | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| sub-main zone length | 231 m | **910 m** | 1,805 m |
| chambers in it | 8 | **29** | 59 |
| properties delivered at its outlet | 18 | **152** | 686 |
| contributing sewer at its outlet | 1,237 m | **4,159 m** | 15,994 m |
| its own length as a share of its catchment | 11.3 % | **~21 %** | 35.2 % |

`research_hierarchy_zone_stats.csv`, `..._submain_catchments.csv`.

**R5 — Spacing and count.** [Likely] One sub main per roughly 4–10 km of package sewer, and
sub mains sit 290 m to 1.6 km apart (median ~480 m centroid-to-centroid).

| Package | sewer km | sub mains | km per sub main |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5A-3 | 3.46 | **0** | — |
| 5A-4 | 5.04 | 1 | 5.0 |
| 5A-2 | 20.01 | 5 | 4.0 |
| 5A-5 | 40.35 | 4 | 10.1 |

`research_hierarchy_packages.csv`, `..._submain_spacing.csv`.

**R6 — Below about 4 km of network a package gets no sub-main tier at all.** [Likely]
5A-3 (3.46 km, 20 lateral zones, 243 accounts) has none: its 20 laterals connect straight to
the trunk, and 8 of the 13 laterals that touch a trunk main anywhere in the whole system are
5A-3's. `research_hierarchy_trunk_joiners.csv`. One observation, so [Likely] not [Certain].

**R7 — Fan-in.** [Certain] 63 zones join the ten sub mains through 67 pipes, 60 of those pipes
being laterals; the median joining zone carries 24 properties. `research_hierarchy_fan_in.csv`, `..._submain_joiners.csv`.

## 3. What makes something a trunk main

**R8 — The trunk is the path from the outfall backwards. It is defined top-down, not by a
threshold.** [Certain]

Four trunk zones exist — `5A-2-TM`, `5A-3-TM`, `5A-4-TM`, `5A-5-TM`, one per package that
has an outfall path. **Only three pipes in the entire 3,266 cross a package boundary, and
all three are trunk-to-trunk**: 5A-3-TM → 5A-2-TM → 5A-4-TM → the outfall manhole, and
5A-5-TM → the plant. `research_hierarchy_package_crossings.csv`.

So a "package trunk main" is simply the reach of the system's single spine that happens to
lie inside that package. That is a naming convention laid over one continuous outfall route.

**R9 — The trunk sits in the low ground, measurably.** [Certain] Ground level of each pipe
minus the median ground level of every pipe within 300 m:

| Tier | p25 | **p50** | p75 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| lateral | −0.42 m | **+0.06 m** | +0.73 m |
| sub main | −0.80 m | **−0.36 m** | +0.10 m |
| **trunk main** | **−1.77 m** | **−1.05 m** | −0.07 m |

`research_hierarchy_tier_relative_elevation.csv`. The naive comparison of raw ground levels
(trunk median 362.5 m vs lateral 342.2 m) says the opposite and is meaningless — it only
reflects which package sits on which hill.

**R10 — Only about 22 things touch the trunk across the whole 101 km.** [Certain] 9 sub
mains and 13 laterals, of which 8 laterals belong to 5A-3, the package with no sub-main
tier. Take 5A-3 out and it is **9 sub mains + 5 laterals = 14** connections over 10.18 km of
trunk. `research_hierarchy_trunk_joiners.csv`.

Expressed scale-free, that is **one trunk connection per 4.6 km of network served** (22 over
101 km), or one per 460 m of trunk. W8 capped its test area at 19–20 joins over 71.6 km of
network — one per 3.6–3.8 km — so **W8's cap was in the right band, but for the wrong reason**:
it was tuned on pumping-station count, not derived. The correct rule is that only sub mains
touch the trunk, plus whatever laterals sit in a sub-district too small to justify one.

**R11 — The trunk carries 15 % of the length.** [Certain] On the labelled packages, trunk
14.8 % / sub main 14.4 % / lateral 70.8 % by length. For comparison, W8's test area measured
5.1 / 18.4 / 66.5 and W8's own design produced 8.4 / 12.2 / 79.3.
`research_hierarchy_tier_shares.csv`.

## 4. The chaining rule

**R12 — A lateral may chain into another lateral, but the chain is short and the flow path
to a main is bounded.** [Certain]

| Labelled packages | p50 | p75 | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| lateral zones crossed before a main | 2 | 3 | 3 | **5** |
| flow-path length from a lateral pipe to the first main | 258 m | 410 m | 722 m | **1,153 m** |

`research_hierarchy_chain_depth.csv`, `..._reach_to_main.csv`.

**R13 — What ends a chain is NOT a load ceiling on the lateral.** [Certain] Compare the last
lateral zone before a main against every other lateral zone:

| At the zone outlet | joins a SM/TM (73 zones) | joins another lateral (509) |
|---|---:|---:|
| properties, p50 | 20 | 9 |
| contributing length, p50 | 544 m | 140 m |
| zone's own length, p50 | 283 m | 80 m |

`research_hierarchy_chain_termination.csv`. The handover zone is bigger, but only by a factor
of two, and the distributions overlap heavily. **Nothing in the data supports "a lateral must
promote once it exceeds N properties".** The chain ends where a sub main happens to run past.

**R14 — Promotion IS predictable from contributing length, but only as a consequence.**
[Likely] Single-threshold classifiers on the labelled packages:

| Predictor | best threshold | balanced accuracy | recall | precision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| contributing **length** | **821 m** | **0.899** | 0.880 | 0.750 |
| contributing pipes | 26 | 0.900 | 0.897 | 0.720 |
| contributing properties | 33 | 0.868 | 0.840 | 0.694 |

Two-level, sweeping both cuts: **sub main at ≥ 1,500 m and trunk at ≥ 16,000 m of
contributing sewer reproduces the designer's own three-way label on 90.8 % of pipes**, and
reproduces the tier lengths to within 2.2 km (predicted 7.94 / 10.11 km trunk / sub main
against actual 10.18 / 9.92). `research_hierarchy_rule_sweep_length.csv`,
`..._rule_sweep_two_level.csv`.

**Contributing length beats contributing properties as a predictor.** That is a real result
and it makes sense: the designer was laying a network, not counting houses.

**R15 — The rule transfers to the package that has no labels.** [Likely] Apply the fitted
1,500 / 16,000 m rule to 5A-1, which the designer never tiered:

| | 5A-1 under the rule | 5A-2…5A-5 under the same rule | 5A-2…5A-5 actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| trunk | 0.74 km (2.3 %) | 7.94 km (11.5 %) | 10.18 km (14.8 %) |
| sub main | 7.59 km (23.5 %) | 10.11 km (14.7 %) | 9.92 km (14.4 %) |
| lateral | 23.91 km (74.1 %) | 50.81 km (73.8 %) | 48.75 km (70.8 %) |

`research_hierarchy_rule_applied_to_5A1.csv`. The lateral share lands at 74.1 % against
73.8 % / 70.8 % — the rule produces a sane hierarchy on a package it was not fitted to.
Its trunk share is low because 5A-1 outfalls to a pumping station rather than running a long
spine.

**R16 — 5A-1 has a de-facto two-system split its IDs do encode.** [Likely] Its zone tokens
carry an `A` or `B` prefix: A = 304 pipes / 9.0 km / 517 properties, B = 819 pipes / 23.2 km
/ 2,187 properties, and only **6 of 1,123 pipes cross between them**.
`research_hierarchy_pkg5A1_prefixes.csv`, `..._pkg5A1_prefix_flow.csv`.

## 5. Diameters by tier — the finding that matters most

**R17 — In the as-built, diameter is a TIER LABEL, not a hydraulic response.** [Certain]

| Tier | OD used | pipes | km | properties contributing, p50 | max |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| lateral | **OD160, 100 %** | 1,675 | 48.75 | 6 | 136 |
| sub main | **OD160, 100 %** | 322 | 9.92 | 99 | 686 |
| trunk main | **OD200, 100 %** | 145 | 4.53 | 421 | 796 |

`research_hierarchy_diameter_by_tier.csv`. Two sizes in the whole network: **58.68 km at

| 0–24 | 1,479 | 0 |
| 25–99 | 351 | 21 |
| 100–399 | 136 | 45 |
| **400–799** | **31** | 79 |

`research_hierarchy_diameter_by_load_band.csv`. Thirty-one pipes carrying 400–799 properties
were laid at OD160.

## 6. Gradients and depths by tier

**R18 — The sub main is the deep, flat pipe. The lateral is shallow and steep. The trunk is
in between and follows the ground.** [Certain] From the real inverts, 2,142 pipes:

| | laid gradient p50 | depth to invert p50 | p95 | deepest | chamber spacing p50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| lateral | **6.03 mm/m** | **1.56 m** | 3.98 m | 7.11 m | 29.6 m |
| sub main | **4.03 mm/m** | **4.17 m** | 6.20 m | 8.35 m | 30.0 m |
| trunk main | **5.19 mm/m** | **3.20 m** | 5.08 m | 5.91 m | 30.0 m |

The deepest pipe in the whole built network — 8.85 m to invert — is a **sub main**, which is
R18 in one number.

`research_hierarchy_gradient_depth_by_tier.csv`.

Two things follow. First, **chamber spacing is tier-independent** — 29–31 m for every tier,
so spacing carries no information about hierarchy, which is exactly why W7's calibration
saw nothing. Second, **the sub main is where the depth is bought**: it sits 2.6 m deeper than
the laterals feeding it and is laid 33 % flatter, which is the price of collecting a cluster
of streets and getting under them all.

**R19 — Gradients are not laid to round steps.** [Certain] Only 44.8 % of pipes sit on a
1 mm/m step and 34.3 % on a 0.05 % step. `research_hierarchy_gradient_rounding.csv`. Our
`SLOPE_STEP = 0.0005` rule is a drawing-office convenience of ours, not a copy of practice.

## 7. Package structure — how a contractor builds it

**R20 — Packages are geographically clean, not interleaved.** [Certain]

| Package | pipes | km | chambers | zones | SM | TM | accounts ≤60 m | hull km² | km/km² | m per account |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5A-1 | 1,123 | 32.25 | 1,124 | 312 | 0 | 0 | 2,738 | 2.36 | 13.7 | 11.8 |
| 5A-2 | 679 | 20.01 | 679 | 72 | 5 | 1 | 588 | 1.96 | 10.2 | 34.0 |
| 5A-3 | 126 | 3.46 | 127 | 21 | 0 | 1 | 243 | 0.30 | 11.6 | 14.2 |
| 5A-4 | 183 | 5.04 | 184 | 23 | 1 | 1 | 368 | 0.35 | 14.4 | 13.7 |
| 5A-5 | 1,155 | 40.35 | 1,156 | 168 | 4 | 1 | 1,292 | 8.66 | 4.7 | 31.2 |

Convex-hull overlap between packages is **zero for every pair except 5A-1/5A-2 (12.8 % /
15.4 %)**. `research_hierarchy_packages.csv`, `..._package_overlap.csv`.

And they are hydraulically clean too: three pipes cross a package boundary, all trunk-to-
trunk (R8). **A package is a self-contained catchment that hands over to the next package
only on the trunk.** That is the buildability rule W11a needs — a package can be tendered,
dug and commissioned without touching its neighbour's laterals. [Certain]

Package size ranges 3.5–40 km and 243–2,738 accounts, so **there is no target package size**
in this data. [Certain]

## 8. What the as-built does at a wadi

**R21 — It mostly stays out, and where it must cross it goes over quickly and shallow rather
than diving under.** [Likely]

Measured on the project's own T50 hazard grid, classes 4/5/6 = wadi (user rule 2026-08-19),
sampled every ~10 m along every pipe:

| Tier | pipes touching a wadi cell | wholly inside | km inside | depth in wadi (p50) | depth outside | gradient in wadi | gradient outside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| lateral | 173 (6.2 %) | 93 (3.3 %) | 5.35 | **1.11 m** | 1.56 m | 6.18 mm/m | 6.03 mm/m |
| sub main | 2 (0.6 %) | 0 | 0.05 | 4.14 m | 4.17 m | 4.43 | 4.03 |
| trunk main | 6 (4.1 %) | 4 (2.7 %) | 0.21 | **2.09 m** | 3.27 m | **10.94 mm/m** | 5.18 mm/m |

`research_hierarchy_wadi_hazard.csv`, `..._wadi_hazard_classes.csv`.

Three readings. **(a)** Sub mains avoid wadis almost completely — 0.6 %, versus 6.2 % for
laterals. **(b)** The trunk crosses at **double the gradient and 1.2 m shallower** than it
runs elsewhere: it takes the short steep route across rather than a deep crossing.
**(c)** No inverted siphon, no drop shaft and no distinctive deep section appears anywhere in
the crossings — nothing in the levels suggests special structures.

**This is a habit to question, not to copy.** A 1.1 m lateral and a 2.1 m trunk inside a
50-year flood channel is a scour and washout exposure, and there is no condition data
(`P_CONDITIO` 0 % populated) to say whether it has already cost them. [Guessing] on whether
it has failed; [Certain] that the depths are what they are.

On the coarser NSA 2 m stream lines, 441 pipes (13.5 %) cross a mapped stream, but those
lines include 2,735 km of order-1 rills and mean little. `research_hierarchy_wadi_crossings.csv`.

## 10. Where the as-built contradicts our design criteria

This is the section worth arguing about. Method: take every levelled built pipe at its own
diameter and its own laid gradient, put today's connected load through it using the
project's own hydraulics and load basis (`hydra.pipe_state`, Merrimack peak factor,
`PLOT_QADF_M3D`, 720 L/d/km infiltration), and compare.
`research_hierarchy_asbuilt_vs_rules.csv`, `..._asbuilt_vs_rules_summary.csv`.

| | lateral (1,670) | sub main (322) | trunk (145) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| OD as built | 160 | 160 | 200 |
| DN our rules would pick, p50 / max | 200 / 200 | 200 / **250** | 200 / **250** |
| **our DN is bigger than theirs** | **100 %** | **100 %** | 45.5 % |
| laid gradient ÷ our required gradient, p50 | 1.21 | **0.82** | 1.21 |
| **flatter than our rules require** | 14.9 % | **65.5 %** | 22.8 % |
| **surcharged at today's load** | 0 % | **17.7 % (57 pipes)** | **36.6 % (53 pipes)** |
| over the d/D limit (G203-p27 Tab 10) | 0 % | 12.4 % | 9.0 % |
| below 0.75 m/s self-cleansing | 99.7 % | 78.6 % | 43.4 % |
| d/D at peak, p50 / p95 | 0.09 / 0.29 | 0.32 / 0.84 | 0.43 / 0.74 |

**Five real disagreements:**

1. **OD160 as a main sewer.** G203-p22 Tab 6 sets **OD200 as the minimum main sewer**; OD160
   is the tertiary size for property connections and riders. Every lateral and every sub main,
   including one carrying 686 properties, is laid at OD160 — **58.68 km of the 63.2 km that
   carries a recorded diameter**, and by inference package 5A-1's further 32.2 km as well.
   [Certain] on the fact, [Certain] on the guideline. This is not a matter of taste: a sub main
   at OD160 has no room to take the growth the 2006 scheme was presumably not asked to allow for.

2. **Sub mains are laid flatter than Table 11 allows.** 65.5 % of them are flatter than our
   required gradient, median ratio 0.82. Against the DN200 Table-11 minimum of 5.00 mm/m the
   sub-main median is 4.03 mm/m — and at OD160 the requirement is steeper still, not flatter.
   [Certain]

3. **At today's connected load, 110 pipes cannot pass peak flow at all.** 57 sub-main and 53
   trunk pipes surcharge. [Likely] — the load is our reconstruction from electricity accounts
   within 60 m, and we do not know which are actually connected. But it agrees with the
   independent arithmetic already in `EXISTING_NETWORK.md`: 4,886 accounts ≈ 4,185 m³/d against
   a plant rated 1,800 m³/d. **Either much of the network is not commissioned, or it is running
   over capacity today.** That is a survey question, and it is the single most important one.

4. **Diameter does not respond to flow at all.** Ours does — that is the whole of Table 11 and
   the tractive-force check. Theirs is a two-value tier label (R17). We should keep ours; the
   as-built is evidence of what happens when you do not.

5. **Velocity.** 99.7 % of laterals run below 0.75 m/s at peak. That is unavoidable at 6
   properties per zone and is not a criticism — it is why G203 has a tractive-force route as
   well as a velocity route. It does mean **the as-built cannot be used as evidence that
   self-cleansing was achieved**, and the CCTV survey should expect siltation in the laterals.

**Three places the as-built is right and our design is not:**

6. **Depth.** The built network's deepest is 8.85 m and 95th percentile 4.93 m. W8's design
   reaches 10.45 m and W10's reaches the 12.0 m cap. They bought that with pumping — 30.8 km
   of rising main — and we buy shallower trenches from a better trunk. Both trades are
   defensible; the comparison is already in `W7/docs/CALIBRATION_vs_EXISTING.md`. Worth
   re-stating because **the deep pipe is always the sub main** (R18): if W10 wants to shed
   depth, that is where to look, not at the trunk.

7. **Manhole spacing.** 29–31 m in every tier. Tested and rejected as something to copy
   (`W7/docs/CALIBRATION_vs_EXISTING.md` §2) — it buys nothing on depth and nearly doubles the
   chamber count. Confirmed here that it also carries no hierarchy information.

8. **Round gradients.** Only 44.8 % of built pipes sit on a 1 mm/m step. Our 0.05 % stepping
   rule is ours, and it should be defended as a drawing-office convenience, not as practice.

## 12. How W11a should assign TIER — algorithm in prose

The current `W10/shp/W10_pipes.shp` has **no TIER field at all** (W8 had one; it was dropped).
20,936 pipes, 1,882.9 km, DN200 on 19,829 of them. Whatever W11a does, it must put the field
back.

The rule below is **top-down and geometric**, because that is what the as-built shows the
designer did. Load thresholds appear only as a guard, never as the primary test.

### Inputs
- the drafted corridor network, noded, with a `parent` pointer per pipe toward the outfall
  (already produced by `p0_topology.py` / `p1_subnetworks.py`)
- accumulated contributing sewer length `UP_LEN` and accumulated properties `UP_PROPS` at
  every pipe (already produced by `p2_sizing.accumulate`)
- the user's Main Pipe alignment
- the road layer, with `dual` and a street-class field
- package/phase polygons, if W11a defines them

### Step 1 — Trunk, from the outfall backwards
Mark as `trunk main` every pipe on the drafted network that lies on the user's Main Pipe
alignment, plus any pipe on the unique downstream path from a designated works or pumping
station inlet. Do not use a load threshold for this. The as-built's four "trunk mains" are
four reaches of one continuous outfall route (R8), and 100 % of them sit within 15 m of a
mapped road and about 1.0 m below the local median ground (R9, R22). Where the alignment is
ambiguous, prefer the route that minimises `ground level − local median over 300 m`.

*Guard:* the trunk should end up at 10–15 % of total length (R11). If it lands below 8 % or
above 20 %, the outfall route is probably wrong, not the rule.

### Step 2 — Lateral zones, bottom-up, one street at a time
Cut the remaining network into zones at every junction. A **lateral zone** is a maximal
unbranched chain of pipes that:
- has exactly **one outlet** and **at most one head chamber** (R1 — 99.3 % / 99.6 %);
- has a bearing spread under about 20° and follows no more than two road centreline features
  (R1 — 72 % of the as-built);
- is **capped at 500 m and 17 chambers** at p95, and **hard-capped at 920 m and 28 chambers**
  (R2, the labelled-package maxima; the largest zone anywhere including 5A-1 is 1,018 m).
  Split a longer run at the nearest junction; never merge two streets into one zone.

Target the p50 shape, not the cap: **5 chambers, 132 m, 4–6 properties, 6 plots fronting.**

### Step 3 — Chain laterals, but only twice
Let a lateral zone drain into another lateral zone. Enforce two limits, both measured:
- **at most 3 lateral zones** between any zone and a sub main or the trunk (as-built p95 = 3,
  max 5 — use 3 as the design limit and 5 as the hard fail);
- **at most 750 m of flow path** through laterals before reaching a main (as-built p95 = 722 m,
  max 1,153 m).

Any zone that would exceed either limit forces a sub main to be created in step 4. This is the
rule W8 did not have; capping the joins onto the trunk was a proxy for it.

### Step 4 — Sub mains, as collector routes
Where a cluster of lateral zones cannot reach the trunk inside the step-3 limits, create a
**sub main**: a continuous route from the head of the cluster to the trunk, running on the
highest street class available and preferring the lower ground (as-built sits ~0.4 m below
local median, R9).

Size and spacing checks, all from R4–R7:
- a sub main is **230–1,800 m long** (p50 910 m) and carries **8–60 chambers**;
- it should be about **20 % of the length of the catchment it drains** (11–35 % observed);
- it should deliver **20–700 properties** at its outlet (p50 152);
- sub mains sit **300 m to 1.6 km apart**;
- expect roughly **one per 4–10 km** of network in the district;
- **a district under about 4 km of sewer gets no sub main** — its laterals join the trunk
  directly (R6);
- **avoid wadi cells entirely** — the as-built puts 0.6 % of sub-main length in one (R21).

Do **not** require load at the head of a sub main. Four of ten as-built sub mains start dry
(R4). A sub main is a route, not a threshold event.

### Step 5 — Only sub mains touch the trunk
Every lateral must reach the trunk through a sub main, with one exception: a district too
small for a sub main under step 4's 4 km test. Expected outcome, scaled off the as-built:
**one trunk connection per 4–5 km of network served** (as-built 22 over 101 km; W8's tuned cap
worked out at one per 3.6–3.8 km, R10). On W10's 1,883 km that is of the order of 400
connections onto an 85.5 km trunk — check it against that band rather than against W8's
absolute number of 19, which belonged to a 71.6 km test area.

### Step 6 — Verify, do not assume
Run these six checks and fail the build on any of them:

| Check | Pass band (as-built basis) |
|---|---|
| trunk share of total length | 10–15 % (R11) |
| sub-main share | 12–18 % (R11) |
| lateral share | 68–76 % (R11) |
| lateral zones that are a simple unbranched path | ≥ 95 % (R1) |
| lateral chain depth, p95 | ≤ 3 (R12) |
| flow path through laterals to a main, p95 | ≤ 750 m (R12) |

And two the as-built fails, which W11a must not inherit:
- **no main sewer below DN200** (G203-p22 Tab 6) — the as-built runs 58.7 km of recorded
  pipe at OD160;
- **no pipe flatter than the governing minimum gradient** — 65.5 % of as-built sub mains are.

### What NOT to carry over
- Diameter as a tier label (R17). Size on flow; the as-built does not.
- 30 m chamber spacing (R18, and W7 §2 which tested and rejected it).
- Shallow, steep wadi crossings (R21).
- W8's join cap onto the trunk. It was a proxy for step 3 and it is no longer needed.
- The "median chain of 11" doctrine. It is an artefact of package 5A-1 (§0).
