# The existing NAMA network, measured

W10 Phase 0.5, 1 September 2026. Everything here is regenerated by
`W10/py/p05_existing.py`; every table has a matching CSV in `W10/run/p05_*.csv`.

Four things in the project record turned out to be wrong. They are at the top because
two of them change what the design can assume.

---

## 1. Read this first: four corrections

**[Certain] The W7/W8 shapefiles are not the whole dataset.** NAMA delivered ESRI
shapefiles *and* KMZ. `W8/py/export_existing.py` converted the KMZ, and the conversion
drops data: it keeps only the first 40 attribute columns, and the KML itself carries
fewer features. The shapefiles in `Data/Received/09-RECEIVED/NAMA/IBRI/WW/SHIP/` are the
complete delivery, and W10 reads those instead.

| | shapefile | KMZ copy in `W7/shp/` | lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| proposed gravity | 129 features, 202.7 km | 55 features, 77.0 km | **74 features, 125.7 km** |
| proposed force main | 8 | 6 | 2 |
| proposed TE main | 8 | 6 | 2 |
| built gravity | 3,267 | 3,267 | none |
| attribute columns | 48 | 41 | `SLOPE`, `MCLASS`, `BED_MATERI`, `CONST_METH`, `LINING_TYP`, `FLOOD_PROT`, `WATER_TABL` |

The four headline lengths in PROJECT-STATE reproduce to the metre from the shapefiles and
**cannot** be reproduced from the KMZ. Whoever measured them used the right source; the
converted layers everything else reads are the wrong one.

**[Certain] Built gravity is 101.1 km, not 111.6 km.** One record inside the gravity
layer — `FEATUREID L012750`, project code `8F-1`, source `cctv`, 10,469 m long — is the
rising main catalogued a second time. Its `DS_MHID` is `5A-1-FL-STP`, "FL" being NAMA's
own tag for force line; 88 % of it lies within 100 m of the FORCELINE polyline and 75 %
within 25 m, and both end at the plant. Counting it as gravity double-counts the asset.
It is kept in the output layer but flagged `IS_DUP = 1`, tier `rising_main`.

**[Certain] Invert levels and diameters *are* recorded — on 65.6 % of the built
network.** The record says none are. The 65.6 % is not scattered; it splits cleanly on
package:

| Package | Pipes | km | Inverts, ground levels, diameter, material |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 5A-1 | 1,123 | 32.25 | **none — 0.0 %** |
| 5A-2 | 679 | 20.01 | 100 % |
| 5A-3 | 126 | 3.45 | 100 % |
| 5A-4 | 183 | 5.04 | 100 % |
| 5A-5 | 1,155 | 40.35 | 100 % |

So 2,143 of 3,266 gravity pipes carry upstream and downstream invert, upstream and
downstream ground level, outside diameter and material. What is universally absent is
`N_DIAMETER` (the nominal-size field) — which is presumably what was checked, since it
reads 0 on every built record while `OUT_DIAMET` carries the real value. **A hydraulic
assessment of the built network is possible today over 68.8 km of it**, which is not what
the R1 report tells the client.

**[Certain] The force main does not run downhill the whole way.** It falls 22.37 m over
9,994 m, so the *average* is downhill at 0.224 %, but the ground is not monotonic. It
drops to **325.40 m at chainage 8,620** and then climbs back to 328.72 m at the plant —
the low point is 3.32 m *below* plant ground. That dip, not the average slope, is what
decides section 5.

## 4. Hierarchy, connectivity and what it drains to

### It is three systems, not one

`US_MHID`/`DS_MHID` and endpoint snapping at 0.5 m agree exactly — same three groups,
same sizes. `W10/run/p05_systems.csv`.

| System | Packages | Pipes | km | Levels? | Outlet | Accounts within 60 m |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| A | 5A-5 | 1,155 | 40.35 | yes | **gravity direct to the STP** | 1,242 |
| B | 5A-1 | 1,123 | 32.25 | **no** | the existing pumping station, then the 9.99 km rising main | 2,583 |
| C | 5A-2 + 5A-3 + 5A-4 | 988 | 28.51 | yes | MH `5A-4-SM-MH917` at 450565, 2567465, then the 8F-1 line | 1,145 |
| | | **3,266** | **101.10** | | | **4,886** |

The three touch each other only at the plant node. **No gravity connection exists between
them** — a design that assumes the built network is one system will be wrong.

System C's outlet sits 670 m north-east of the pumping station and its outfall line
converges on the rising main. [Likely] C is tributary to the same pumping station and the
8F-1 record lumps the 670 m gravity tail together with the rising main; [Guessing] on
whether there is a second, separate pumping arrangement, because **no pumping-station
record was supplied at all** (see section 6).

### Gradients and depths as built

Derived from the inverts, on the 2,143 pipes that carry them. `W10/run/p05_gradients_mm_per_m.csv`.

| | mm/m | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5th percentile | 2.88 | 0.29 |

No adverse gradients; 5 pipes are laid dead flat. Depth to invert: median 1.82 m upstream

6 m. The built network is far shallower than W8's design (deepest 10.45 m).

Two compliance observations, both [Certain] against the criteria in `W8/py/sewnet/criteria.py`:

- **1,997 of 2,142 pipes with a recorded diameter are OD160.** G203-p22 Tab 6 sets OD200
  as the minimum *main* sewer; 160 is the tertiary size. 93 % of the levelled built
  network is laid at a size that today's guideline allows only for property connections
  and riders.
- **549 of 2,143 pipes are flatter than 0.50 %**, which is the Table-11 minimum for
  DN200. At OD160 they are flatter still relative to what self-cleansing needs.

Neither is a reason to condemn a 2006 asset. Both are reasons the survey must measure
performance rather than assume it.

## 5. Is the pumping station avoidable?

The question: the built pumping station at 449899.6, 2567301.7 (ground 351.1 m) lifts
system B into a 9,994 m rising main that falls 22.37 m to the plant. If the ground falls
the whole way, why is it pumped?

### The ground profile

Sampled every 10 m off `IBRI_0p5_VRT2.vrt`. `W10/run/p05_forcemain_profile.csv`.

| Chainage | Ground |
|---:|---:|
| 0 (pumping station) | 351.09 |
| 380 (high point) | 352.25 |
| 2,410 | 349.76 (local hump) |
| 6,000 | 332.94 |
| **8,620 (low point)** | **325.40** |
| 9,994 (plant) | 328.72 |

The route rises 1.1 m before it falls, and — the part that matters — it drops **3.32 m
below plant ground** at chainage 8,620 and climbs back. The established note that it
"runs downhill 22.4 m at 0.224 % average" is true only as an average.

### The answer

Method: walk downstream from the pumping station hugging the minimum-cover ceiling
(1.30 m to crown, G203-p33), dropping at the Table-11 minimum gradient wherever the
ground will not fall that fast on its own. That is the *shallowest* pipe that still
self-cleanses, so its depth is a lower bound and its arrival level at the plant is the
highest achievable. `W10/run/p05_gravity_check.csv`.

| DN | S min | Arrives at | vs. plant inlet 322.99 | Max depth | Length > 12 m | v full | Peak capacity |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 0.155 % | 321.47 | −1.52 m | 9.33 m | 0 m | 0.76 | 20.5 L/s · 1,771 m³/d |
| 600 | 0.125 % | 321.78 | −1.21 m | 9.25 m | 0 m | 0.76 | 42.6 L/s · 3,679 m³/d |
| **700** | **0.100 %** | **322.03** | **−0.96 m** | **9.20 m** | **0 m** | **0.75** | **76.7 L/s · 6,623 m³/d** |
| 800 | 0.085 % | 322.13 | −0.86 m | 9.21 m | 0 m | 0.76 | 128.5 L/s · 11,102 m³/d |
| 900 | 0.075 % | 322.17 | −0.82 m | 9.25 m | 0 m | 0.76 | 203.0 L/s · 17,535 m³/d |
| 1200 | 0.075 % | 321.87 | −1.12 m | 9.55 m | 0 m | 0.92 | 693.6 L/s · 59,930 m³/d |

**[Likely] Yes, the pumping station is avoidable — but it costs about a metre of plant
inlet level, not a pump.** A gravity main on this exact alignment works hydraulically:
maximum depth 9.2–9.6 m at any size, comfortably inside the 12 m recommendation, and
nothing at all deeper than 12 m. Self-cleansing is met at every size by construction.
The single obstacle is that it arrives **0.8 to 1.5 m below the invert the existing
5A-5 trunk arrives at (322.99 m)**. The dip at chainage 8,620 forces the pipe down, and
from there it cannot climb back to the existing inlet.

Why that is a soft constraint rather than a hard one: **this project is designing the
plant.** The 1,800 m³/d works is 20 years old and 2.3× oversubscribed by its own
catchment (below), and NAMA's own SUREKHA proposal already puts a 29,038 m³/d plant
117 m to the south. The inlet level of a works that does not exist yet is a design
variable. Committing to a permanent pumping station and its whole-life energy and
maintenance cost, to avoid setting a new inlet works one metre lower, is very unlikely
to survive the NPV comparison at 5 % over 25 years.

Sizing, if it is taken forward: system B alone is 2,583 accounts ≈ 2,212 m³/d Q_adf
today, so **DN700 at 0.10 % is about right for the present load and DN900 covers
substantial growth** (17,535 m³/d peak). Capacities in the table are peak flows at the
d/D limit (0.65 for DN ≤ 350, 0.50 above), Colebrook-White with kₛ = 1.5 mm.

**Confidence.** [Certain] on the ground profile, the depths and the arrival levels — they
are measured off the 0.5 m terrain and computed with the project's own validated
hydraulics module. [Likely] on the conclusion, with two named caveats: the 322.99 m plant
inlet comes from a single NAMA record on a 5,648 m schematic line rather than from
survey, and the pipe must pass under whatever the rising main and the road corridor
already occupy along the same alignment, which nothing in this dataset describes. Both
are survey items. [Guessing] on why it was pumped in 2006 — the likeliest reason is that
the 5A-1 catchment sits high and the 2006 scheme sized the plant for 1,800 m³/d, so a
small pumping station was cheaper than a deep 10 km trunk. That reasoning does not carry
over to a scheme designed for ~49,700 m³/d.
