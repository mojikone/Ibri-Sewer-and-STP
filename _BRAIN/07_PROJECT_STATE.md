## 2. Settled engineering doctrine (user-agreed, binding for design work)
1. **Load allocation** (agreed 2026-08-15): *plots at saturation size the pipes; capped-and-spilled zone totals at dated years size the STP phases; the two meet only at trunk nodes.*
   - Pipes/civil: EVERY plot (built + future + unparceled buildings) at full saturation load (properties × OR × 171 l/c/d), accumulated with PF. No timing.
   - Dated years: zone totals only — R0 projection capped at zone ceiling (plots × OR); surplus spills to adjacent zones proportional to vacancy (A2 model). Never per-plot-per-year.
   - Phased elements: STP trains, pumps, force-main duty equipment (M&E ~20 yr life). Buried civil = saturation.
   - Early-years check: self-cleansing (0.75 m/s / tractive force, G203-p26-27) verified at start-year flows.
   - Farms: **the agricultural USE carries no sewage load (they are TE customers), but DWELLINGS on a farm plot do** (narrowed 2026-08-19 — 1,947 CLASS=A plots carry 3,366 domestic electricity accounts, so there are houses on them). **Sharpened 2026-08-30 into a testable rule**: an agricultural meter is an irrigation pump. On 172 of 319 agricultural plots NAMA bills a household tariff *alongside* the farm tariff, so dwelling and pump are metered separately. Therefore an agricultural meter on a plot that already carries a domestic meter adds **nothing**, and an agricultural-only plot carries **no dwelling** (147 plots ≈ 782 people ≈ 0.67 %). Recompute the split when the clean plot layer lands.
1b. **LOAD BASIS — LOCKED 2026-08-30.** **Tier A ratios set the VOLUME; land use sets the PLACEMENT.** Domestic = counted domestic accounts × OR × LPCD; non-domestic and governmental = +22 % / +14 % of domestic (Tab 11, Adh Dhahirah); that volume lands on commercial and government plots identified by account tariff rather than being smeared across population. **Table 12 is NOT used** — it is priced in pupils, beds, employees and floor area, and the electricity file contains no land use, no floor area and no consumption at all, so the §7.3.2 "shall" condition is unmet in substance. Never apply Table 12 and the ratios together. Four streams stay additive outside the ratios because the guideline excludes them by name: identified projects (the industrial estate), special consumption (labour camps), blue and yellow tankers, and private wells. **Tanker water DOES generate sewage** at the same 85 % return rate as domestic (Tab 19). This supersedes the ambiguous 2026-08-19 wording, which read as though tariff replaced the ratios. Detail: `02_DESIGN_CRITERIA.md` §11.1 and `W9/docs/INFERENCE_REGISTER.md`.
1c. **Occupancy rate = 5.32** (derived 2026-08-30; the earlier 5.0 was a placeholder, never a decision). Measured as 2024 settlement population ÷ domestic properties with both clipped to the same 25 settlements; Ibri town alone 6.21. Validated by coverage rather than by the guideline formula, because R0 publishes LPCD 163.5 but never the consumption or customer counts behind it: settlements hold 63.4 % of wilayat population, meters represent 65.5 % of properties implied at OR 5.32, agreeing within 2.0 points. Four settlements unusable pending boundary review (Sayh Al Masarrat 1.07, Al Jahli 1.30, Al Akheedar 1.64, Satwah 10.12).
1d. **Accuracy is DIRECTIONAL.** One population cannot serve all three uses. Pipe sizing and STP staging fail on under-estimates; the **self-cleansing and early washing schedule fails on OVER-estimates** — assume more early flow than exists and the pipes are declared self-scouring while silting, and maintenance is told to stop washing too soon. Size capacity on the high case, set the wash schedule on the low case, and carry the connection ratio (0.51 in 2024 → 0.61 by 2028, R0) explicitly in the third.
1e. **OPTIONS DOCTRINE (settled 2026-08-31).** **Three options per system, not three overall** — three for the sewer network, three for the treated effluent network, three for the STP, and the guideline says "each STP" so three per plant if more than one is proposed. **Lifting stations are NOT a separate set**: their number is a consequence of each network layout and they are compared within it. Each set follows the three archetypes named at G201-p95 (sustainability-led / international best practice / established local practice); the guideline names them and stops, so the design-terms interpretation in `W9/report` Section 21.2 and in README is **ours**, offered for NWS comment, never presented as a guideline requirement. Comparison is over **25 years at a 5 % discount rate** against **seven** criteria: total lifetime cost; sustainability (carbon, circular economy, NBS); social development and in-country value; adaptability and resilience; operability; constructability; environmental impact. **NWS sets the weights.** Sensitivity varies exactly three things: the weighting, the discount rate, and the input design criteria. **Tie-break: within 10 % on total lifetime cost the greener option is adopted** — three options over 25 years often land inside 10 %, so the sustainability scoring frequently decides the outcome. Constraint: constructed wetlands are limited to works of about 500 m3/d (G203-p101), so nature-based treatment serves outlying settlements or polishing, not the main plant.
2. **Saturation** = no more developable plots. Zone-level ceilings bite inside the design horizon (IBRI ≈2038); whole boundary saturates ≈2062–2070 at OR 6.0; beyond that R0 projections are physically unhousable (407k surplus by 2100) — pending NCSI/MoHUP resolution.
3. **Maximum COVER is 12 m — with two bounded exceptions, created 2026-09-02.** The rule was "12 m with no exceptions" (settled 2026-08-19 after the user found 21.3 m chambers passing the audit). The engineer reversed that on 2026-09-02, deliberately and on the record: the limit is measured as **cover** per G203-p33, and it may be passed if **either** the cover recovers within **500 m**, **or** the run reaches the outfall — works or an existing lifting station — within **1,000 m**. Both exits are bounded by DISTANCE, not depth, because the guideline gives no depth (that is a pipe-manufacturer question) and defines "prohibitive" only by comparison with a station's cost, which NWS have not confirmed. **Everything past 12 m is flagged individually** with its depth, its length, the rule that allowed it, and what it waits on. Full rule: `08_DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY.md` §5.1c. The original wording follows, because the reason it was written still stands (G203-p33; settled 2026-08-19 after the user
   found 21.3 m chambers passing the audit). The limit applies to every chamber AND to the trench
   between chambers. It may never be relaxed by calling an area an "SLS pocket" — that exemption
   caused the failure and has been deleted from the audit. Where a gravity sewer would pass 12 m,
   a **pumping station goes in before that point**: the sewage is lifted and the pipe restarts at
   normal cover, so the next stretch runs by gravity. Pumps are not to be removed by digging
   deeper; the route is re-searched first, and whatever pumping is left is real.
   - The rising main from each station is sized on the **pump duty** (wet well emptied faster than
     it fills), keeping 0.75-3.0 m/s (G203-p50 8.1) — not on the arriving gravity flow.
   - Stations within 1.5 km of one another are cascade candidates (rule 9); land acquisition makes
     the station COUNT the thing to minimise.
4. **A sewer network is a HIERARCHY, not a flat set of branches** (learned from the as-built, 2026-08-23). Properties feed riders, riders feed laterals, laterals feed laterals, and only collectors reach the trunk. In NAMA's built network 91% of laterals drain into another lateral and only ~16 things touch the trunk. Designing every catchment its own path to the main pipe produces a network no contractor would build. Every pipe carries a `TIER` field — trunk main / sub main / lateral — in the as-built's own vocabulary.
5. **Guideline values are quoted from the source, never from memory** (user, 2026-08-23: "no guessing can be tolerated"). Verification on 23 Aug corrected three long-standing loose quotes: the depth rule is a RECOMMENDATION of "approximately 10-12 m" COVER whose stated trigger for pumping is excavation COST, not a hard 12 m to invert; G203 Table 6 carries a 45 m maximum lateral length that the code declares but never enforces; and Merrimack is stated only for catchments over 100 properties.
6. **Inlet angle** (G203-p30, "shall"): no pipe may arrive at a chamber pointing against the flow.
   Where a street meets at a bad angle a **bend chamber** goes a few metres short of the junction
   so the turn is made in two halves. Where there is no room (2 m plot clearance, 3 m chamber
   clearance) the junction is flagged for a purpose-made chamber with a curved channel.
7. **Stub-outs**: capped connections at future-plot frontage, sized for that area's saturation flow; usually DN200 minimum governs.
8. Flow chain per **TUTORIALS/T01**; design constraints per **TUTORIALS/T02** (every value read back from the guideline PDF with its page) (Rev 2, 47 pp, docx/pdf) — the teaching reference with every value page-cited; reconciliation register vs Inception R0 inside.

1f. **FINANCIAL METHOD (settled 2026-09-01).** **NPV and life-cycle cost at 5 % over 25 years decide; payback is reported but does not.** For a sewerage scheme most of the benefit is cost avoided — tanker haulage, septic emptying, deferring a new plant — rather than revenue earned, and treated effluent is the only genuine volumetric income, capped by irrigation demand rather than by what the plant treats. A payback computed on revenue alone would misstate the case. There is **no NPV or life-cycle-cost equation anywhere in G201/202/203**: the requirement is stated (G1-p57 §7.1 names NPV and the 25-year period; p95–96 fix 5 % and the MCDA), the method is not, so cite **ISO 15686-5**. Nor are there any unit rates, OPEX rules, tariffs or revenue treatment in the guidelines — which is why the NWS pre-investment appraisals matter as the only NWS-sourced cost data. **CAPEX structure adopted from them** (unit rate × quantity by diameter, reservoir/plant per m³ or m³/d, then preliminaries and contingency), with rates escalated to a stated base date. **OPEX is built bottom-up from duty**, never as a percentage of capital: for wastewater, energy (pumping and aeration) and sludge dominate, and neither follows capital value; M&E replacement falls inside the 25 years and is carried in the year it occurs. **Gravity sewers are priced by diameter AND depth band** — excavation dominates, so a flat rate per diameter is wrong. Every option is costed as designed, including its own pumping, storage and energy; a costed **Option 0 (do nothing)** is the baseline. Figure and full method: `W9/py/make_appraisal_figure.py`, report §21.3 and §35, T03_R01 §20.

1g. **WHAT NOT TO INHERIT FROM THE PIADs (2026-09-01).** Two NWS pre-investment appraisals were read end to end against their own workbooks; eleven defects are traced in `W9/analysis/W9_PIAD_financial_review.md`. The four that would bite us: revenue billed on a **peak-day** volume **including losses** with the domestic/non-domestic split **inverted** (overstates annual income 2.09×, halves every payback); **alternatives that were never independently costed** — the same sheet as the preferred option with one pipe length changed, sharing its reservoir and omitting their own pumping; **OPEX as a percentage of capital with energy and labour at zero**, leaving 78 % of the total in a single rule charging chlorination at 3 % of reservoir capital; and **annual OPEX × 25 called a lifetime cost**, which overstates present value by ~2.05× at 5 %. Also: the recommended option was never risk-assessed, and the 2019 and 2023 unit rates are identical to the digit despite a stated basis of "recent tender prices". Rebuilt on the guideline basis the ranking survived (winner ahead by 27.8 % on life-cycle cost) — so the decisions were right and the magnitudes were not, which matters for any budget or tariff use.
